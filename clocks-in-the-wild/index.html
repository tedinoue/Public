Clocks in the Wild

Field reports from synths in the wild

This is a follow-up to "What 'AI Can't Read Clocks' Actually Means". The original piece ran the eight stimuli below through five frontier models via API at controlled prompts. This page asks the same question of synths in the wild — your synth, in whatever interface and configuration you actually use it.

Pick one or more clocks below, copy a prompt (or use your own), paste the image and prompt into your synth, then come back and record the time it gave. You don't have to test all eight clocks or all six prompts — fill in only what you tried.

This is a field report, not a controlled study. Self-selection, prior context, and re-prompting all bias the data. We're collecting it anyway because the variety of in-the-wild configurations is itself the interesting signal.

How this works (quick read)
  1. Open a synth in whatever interface you normally use.
  2. Click a clock below to expand it. Right-click the image and "Save image as…" or click to view full-size.
  3. Copy one of the six prompts (or use your own — note "custom" in the answer if so).
  4. Paste the prompt and the image into your synth, ask for the time.
  5. Record the time the synth gave in the input box for that prompt. Optionally paste its full response.
  6. Repeat for as many clock+prompt combinations as you like, then submit at the bottom.

The six prompts come from the original experiment. S1 is the naive baseline (no system prompt). S2 is a combined "methodical clockmaker" system prompt. S3a–S3d isolate single components of S2 (methodology only, feature-check only, anti-reasoning, persona+stakes). For S2–S3d, set them as system prompt if your interface supports it; otherwise paste them at the top of your message followed by the image and "What time does this clock show?"
